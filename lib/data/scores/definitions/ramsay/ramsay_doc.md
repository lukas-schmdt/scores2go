# Ramsay Sedation Scale

The Ramsay Scale was one of the first sedation scoring systems and remains widely used in clinical practice. It describes six levels of sedation/arousal, ranging from anxious and agitated (Level 1) to deeply sedated with no response to stimulation (Level 6).

## Scale

| Level | State | Clinical Signs |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | Awake – anxious/agitated | Patient restless, agitated, or anxious |
| 2 | Awake – cooperative | Patient cooperative, orientated, and tranquil |
| 3 | Awake – responds to commands | Patient responds to commands only |
| 4 | Asleep – brisk response | Brisk response to light glabellar tap or loud auditory stimulus |
| 5 | Asleep – sluggish response | Sluggish response to light glabellar tap or loud auditory stimulus |
| 6 | Asleep – no response | No response to glabellar tap or loud auditory stimulus |

## Assessment

- **Levels 2–3** are typically the **target** for procedural sedation and most ICU patients requiring light sedation.
- **Levels 4–5** represent deeper sedation, used for procedures requiring the patient to remain still or for patients on mechanical ventilation.
- **Level 6** reflects very deep sedation or an over-sedated state that warrants immediate review.
- **Level 1** indicates inadequate sedation — consider additional analgesia or anxiolytics.

:::warning Limitations
The Ramsay Scale does not assess agitation in detail and has limited reliability compared to newer tools such as the RASS. For routine ICU monitoring the RASS is generally preferred, as it better characterises agitation levels and has stronger psychometric validation.
:::

## Compared to RASS

| Ramsay | Approximate RASS equivalent |
|--------|-----------------------------|
| 1 | +1 to +4 |
| 2 | 0 |
| 3 | −1 |
| 4 | −2 to −3 |
| 5 | −3 to −4 |
| 6 | −5 |
