Nu-DESC

The Nursing Delirium Screening Scale is a brief, nurse-rated tool for delirium screening. Five items rated 0–2 give a total of 0–10; a total of 2 or more is a positive screen. It is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

🇩🇪 Deutsch

Overview

The Nu-DESC was developed by Gaudreau and colleagues as a fast, systematic tool that lets nurses screen for delirium continuously, based on observations made during routine care. In the original study of 146 hospitalised patients, a cutoff of ≥ 2 gave a sensitivity of 85.7 % and a specificity of 86.8 % against the CAM [1]. The scale was derived from the Confusion Rating Scale, with an additional item for psychomotor retardation, so that it also captures the hypoactive presentation.

Five behavioural items are rated 0, 1 or 2 by the nurse on the basis of observations over the current shift, not only at the moment of assessment. Completion takes roughly 1–2 minutes and requires no formal cognitive testing of the patient. A guideline-conform German translation was published by Lütz and colleagues in 2008 [2].

Scale Items & Rating

ItemDefinition
1. DisorientationVerbal or behavioural manifestation of not being oriented to time or place, or misperceiving persons in the surroundings.
2. Inappropriate behaviorBehaviour inappropriate to the place and/or person, e.g. pulling at tubes or dressings, trying to get out of bed when contraindicated.
3. Inappropriate communicationCommunication inappropriate to the place and/or person, e.g. incoherence, lack of communication, nonsensical or unintelligible speech.
4. Illusions / hallucinationsSeeing or hearing things that are not there; distortion of visual objects.
5. Psychomotor retardationDelayed responsiveness, few or no spontaneous actions or words, e.g. delayed reaction when prodded, or patient cannot be aroused.
RatingMeaning (per item)
0Absent
1Mild or occasional
2Marked, frequent or intense
Rate each item on the basis of observations over the current shift (e.g. 8–12 h). The five item ratings are summed (maximum 10).

Score Interpretation

Total scoreInterpretation
0Negative screen. A negative screen does not exclude delirium; sensitivity is limited, especially for hypoactive delirium. Reassess each shift.
1Negative at the standard cutoff (≥ 2). A total of 1 is positive at the more sensitive cutoff of ≥ 1 validated in the recovery room (sensitivity 95 %, specificity 87 %) [3]. Consider closer observation and confirm clinically if suspicion exists.
≥ 2Positive screen — delirium suspected. Confirm with a diagnostic assessment (e.g. CAM / CAM-ICU or DSM-5 criteria) and look for causes.
No severity grading of the Nu-DESC total has been validated; a higher total should not be read as a validated measure of delirium severity.

Scientific Validity & Limitations

The Nu-DESC is consistently reported to have high specificity, but its sensitivity varies considerably and is often limited outside the original cohort. In a comparison of three screening tools on a surgical ward, the Nu-DESC was the most sensitive of the three [4]. In contrast, a study of postoperative elderly patients reported a sensitivity of only 32 % at the ≥ 2 cutoff [5]. In 405 hospitalised inpatients, sensitivity and specificity were 42 % and 98 % at ≥ 2, and 67 % and 93 % at ≥ 1 [6]. A 2020 meta-analysis of 11 studies (2,245 patients) found a pooled sensitivity of 68.6 % and a pooled specificity of 89.4 % [7].

Limitations to keep in mind:

  • Screening, not diagnosis: a positive result indicates suspected delirium and should be confirmed with a diagnostic assessment; a negative result does not exclude delirium.
  • Hypoactive delirium: the scale is weaker for hypoactive presentations, which are easily overlooked.
  • Dependence on observation: ratings rely on the nurse's observations across the shift, so they depend on observation time, patient contact and rater experience.
  • No validated severity grading: the total score is not a validated measure of delirium severity.
  • Choice of cutoff: the standard cutoff of ≥ 2 favours specificity; the lower cutoff of ≥ 1 increases sensitivity at the cost of specificity [3][6].
  • Other instruments: where an ICU team already uses a confirmatory or alternative tool such as the CAM-ICU or the ICDSC, the Nu-DESC does not replace it; the tools differ in design and in the settings in which they were validated.
